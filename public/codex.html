The core concept

Every player picks one industry — timber, stone, clay, or iron. Each industry is the same set of building "slots," reskinned with that industry's resource. A player's identity is which column they got; the shape of their tech tree is identical to everyone else's.

On top of the industry columns sits a shared Common set (housing, roads, services, power, sanitation, police, transport…) that every player has identically.

The economy is multiplayer by design: the top industry tier is not self-sufficient — it needs another industry's good — so players must trade. See Trade Ring.

Industry template — the 11 slots

Every industry fills these slots. To add a new industry, instantiate all 11 with the new resource, mint the resources below, and splice into the trade ring.

Industry spine (5 buildings)

  • T1 Resource extractor — mines the raw industry good (placed on resource tiles; walker hauls it).
  • T2 Cross-converter — raw → a support good consumed by the next industry's T4 (the trade ring).
  • T2 Industry processor — raw → T2 good.
  • T3 Industry processor — T2 → T3 good.
  • T4 Cross-recipe processor — T3 good + another industry's support good → an industrial luxury (high-tier housing prereq + premium trade good).

Food spine (4 buildings)

  • T1 Food extractor — placed on its food terrain tile → a raw food.
  • T2 Food processor — raw food → basic processed food.
  • T3 Food processor (staple) — → a second basic food (iron's Bakery→bread; timber/stone/clay's Cookhouse/Galley/Pottage House).
  • T3 Luxury food processor — → a luxury food (gates tier 6+ housing).

Boosters (2 buildings)

  • T1 Resource booster — +25% to adjacent extractors (range 2, MAX not stacked).
  • T1 Food booster — +25% to adjacent food extractors.

Resources a new industry mints (~9)

  • 1 raw industry good · 1 T2 good · 1 T3 good · 1 support good · 1 industrial luxury
  • 1 raw food · 1 basic food (T2) · 1 staple food (T3) · 1 luxury food
Adding a new industry also means choosing its place in the trade ring (which support good it produces, and which neighbor's support good its T4 consumes). A 5th industry extends the ring from a 4-cycle to a 5-cycle.

The trade ring (why it's multiplayer)

Each industry's T4 needs the support good made by another industry's cross-converter. No one can finish their tech tree alone.

TIMBER needs lime → STONE needs glass → CLAY needs nails → IRON needs charcoal → TIMBER ↺
  • Timber makes charcoal → consumed by Iron's Engineer's Workshop
  • Stone makes lime → consumed by Timber's Cabinetmaker
  • Clay makes glass → consumed by Stone's Architect
  • Iron makes nails → consumed by Clay's Mosaic Workshop

High-tier housing (tiers 7–8) also requires industrial luxuries from multiple industries, and tier 8 needs all four at once — forcing a fully-traded city.

Systems & problems

Per-tile and per-player metrics the city has to manage. Most are computed each production tick on the server.

😊 Happiness

Drives population & worker capacity (±1 citizen/min toward/away from a happiness=50 pivot, floor 5). Fed by services, desirability; hurt by crime/pollution.

🏭 Pollution

Production buildings emit (pollution_emit/radius); parks dampen. Crosses parcel borders — your neighbor's pollution lands on you. Feeds desirability.

🗑️ Waste

Buildings emit waste_emit; sanitation coverage clears it. Uncovered → player waste climbs toward 100.

🚨 Crime

crime_emit vs police crime_reduction over a coverage radius. Uncovered tiles read high-risk on the heatmap.

✨ Desirability

City base + service coverage − pollution (desirability_bonus/radius). Gates growth & migration.

⚡ Power

power_output (plants) vs power_load (buildings). Demand > capacity = brownout pressure.

🚦 Congestion / noise

noise_emit/radius + road throughput by road_tier. Heavy industry on weak roads spikes congestion.

🏥 Health

Clinics / Hospital coverage drives player health; interacts with pollution & waste.

🎓 Education

School / Library coverage drives education (and unlocks higher housing).

📈 Migration / productivity

migration_rate (inflow from desirability/happiness) and productivity (output multiplier from services).

Invariants — the review checklist

When we change buildings/resources, check the change against these:

  • Parallelism: every industry fills the same 11 slots. If one gets a building, all four do.
  • Trade ring closed: every industry produces exactly one support good and its T4 consumes exactly one (a different industry's). The ring has no dead ends.
  • Food gate: each industry can satisfy the basic housing food gate locally (raw food + processed food are is_food), and has exactly one luxury food (is_luxury_food) for high-tier housing.
  • Common stays common: housing/road/services/power/sanitation/police/transport/tax are industry_key='common' and never industry-specific.
  • New category checklist: adding a category (not just a building) touches 7+ places — see the new_building_category_checklist memory.
